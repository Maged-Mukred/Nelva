# NELVA UX Wireframe Update v2.0

**Status:** Proposed UX mapping for approval  
**Content source:** NELVA Website Content Architecture & Copy Draft v2.0 — Approved Content Foundation  
**Scope:** Responsive wireframe update only; no high-fidelity design or implementation

---

## 1. UX Objective

Update the existing wireframe so the website clearly expresses Nelva's current
positioning:

> International market development for technology manufacturers.

This is an adaptation of the existing Nordic Structuralist system, not a full
restart. The update must shift emphasis away from the former engineering-company
structure and towards:

- International market development
- Manufacturer Representation
- Market assessment and qualification
- Commercial relationship development
- Technical & Commercial Coordination
- Payment and self-service technology experience

No engineering identity, engineering-service gateway, or technical-delivery
claim should remain in the information architecture.

## 3. Homepage Wireframe

### H01 — Hero

**Purpose:** Establish value, audience, and action within five seconds.

**Desktop composition:**

- 12-column asymmetric grid
- Primary label in the upper-left content field: International market development
- Secondary foundation line directly below or beside it: Based in Espoo, Finland
- Headline spans approximately 9–10 columns
- Supporting copy begins around column 5, creating an editorial offset
- Primary and secondary CTAs sit directly below the supporting copy
- A compact specialist line follows the actions: Payment technology / Manufacturer Representation / Technical & Commercial Coordination

**Mobile composition:**

- Value label first, location second
- Headline follows without excessive vertical delay
- Supporting copy limited to a readable measure
- Primary CTA full width
- Secondary CTA as an underlined text link
- Specialist line becomes a compact stacked list or two-row strip

**Change from previous wireframe:** The first viewport no longer prioritises an
engineering-company identity or decorative technical scale. It prioritises the
market-development proposition and its credible differentiators.

### H02 — Market Challenge

**Purpose:** Explain relevance without becoming a long market-entry article.

**Layout:** Dark editorial section with one dominant headline and two short text
blocks. The first identifies the difficulty; the second explains Nelva's role.

Avoid cards, icons, or a long list of problems.

### H03 — What Nelva Does

**Purpose:** Provide four gateways to the Capabilities page.

**Layout:** Numbered editorial rows rather than equal SaaS cards.

1. Market Assessment and Target Identification
2. Customer and Partner Qualification
3. Commercial Relationship Development & Manufacturer Representation
4. Technical & Commercial Coordination

The third row receives slightly greater visual weight because Manufacturer
Representation is a current contracting capability. Every row links to the
relevant anchored section on `/capabilities`.

### H04 — How Nelva Works

**Purpose:** Show continuity without repeating the detailed process.

**Desktop:** One horizontal sequence:

Assess → Identify → Qualify → Develop → Coordinate → Advance

**Mobile:** Vertical sequence with short labels only.

Include one scope sentence and one boundary statement. Full descriptions remain
on the Capabilities page.

### H05 — Industry Focus

**Purpose:** Show differentiated depth of experience.

**Composition:** Deliberately unequal three-part layout.

- Payment Technology: dominant area, approximately half the section
- Retail & Self-Service Technology: secondary area
- Broader Technology Markets: smallest and visibly labelled as selective

Do not use three equal cards. The hierarchy must communicate that Payment
Technology represents Nelva's strongest specialist area before the visitor
reads the descriptions.

### H06 — Experience and Finland-Based Foundation

**Purpose:** Establish credibility without making the company appear older or
larger than it is.

**Layout:** Controlled blue section with:

- Company headline
- Clear new-company statement
- More-than-ten-years founder-experience statement
- Short experience categories
- Link to Company

Do not place the full founder profile on Home.

### H07 — Final Contact CTA

**Purpose:** Create one clear next step.

**Layout:** Open editorial composition with a large left-aligned headline,
short qualifying copy, and one CTA to Contact.

Until the email is confirmed, the Contact route may exist in the wireframe but
the new website must not be published with a non-functional final conversion
path.

## 5. Capabilities Page Wireframe

### CA01 — Page Hero

- Headline and concise scope statement
- Small process index showing the page's coverage

### CA02–CA04 — Research and Qualification

Group the first three capabilities into a connected opening sequence:

- Market Assessment
- Target Identification
- Customer and Partner Qualification

Use numbered sections with progressive disclosure, not separate service cards.

### CA05 — Manufacturer Representation

Give this section a distinct dark or blue treatment because it is a current,
contractable service.

Structure:

- What it is
- What Nelva may do
- What it does not automatically authorise
- Contextual CTA: Discuss a representation requirement

### CA06–CA07 — Relationship and Coordination

Place Commercial Relationship Development and Technical & Commercial
Coordination in adjacent but distinct sections.

The coordination section must visually connect multiple parties without using a
fabricated systems diagram. A simple labelled relationship line may show:

Manufacturer ↔ Nelva ↔ Relevant market participant

This communicates a real coordination role and must not imply that Nelva is a
technical platform or integrator.

### CA08 — Opportunity Follow-Up

Use a concise checklist and boundary statement.

### CA09 — Distributor and System-Integrator Client Context

Visually separate this section from channel-development content. Label it
clearly: When distributors or system integrators engage Nelva as clients.

### CA10 — Detailed Working Process

Present all six stages with full descriptions near the end of the page.

### CA11 — Final CTA

One CTA covering market development and representation.

## 8. Responsive Behaviour

- Desktop grid: 12 columns
- Tablet grid: 8 columns
- Mobile: 4-column visual rhythm with single-column reading flow
- Touch targets: minimum 48px
- Page headings must not be hidden beneath the sticky header
- Editorial rows stack without changing their hierarchy
- Industry priority remains visible on small screens through order, scale, and labels
- The six-stage process changes orientation, not content order
- Body copy should remain between approximately 16–19px depending on context
- Avoid horizontal scrolling and fixed-width content blocks

---

## 9. High-Fidelity Design Gate

Before high-fidelity design begins, confirm:

1. This UX mapping reflects the approved content hierarchy.
2. Manufacturer Representation receives the correct prominence.
3. Payment Technology remains visibly stronger than the other industry areas.
4. The founder section remains concise.
5. The CTA flow is acceptable while the official email remains pending.

Once approved, high-fidelity design should proceed in this order:

1. Homepage
2. Shared header, navigation, footer, and CTA system
3. Capabilities
4. Industries
5. Company
6. Contact
